A Library of Plans, Not One Plan

In Richmond, a Just Transition looks like food grown and shared by neighbors. Land and energy in community hands. Worker-owned cooperatives. Real democratic say over what happens here, and safety built on belonging, not punishment.

Transition is the work of moving toward an economy based in care and away from the extraction that's exploited this city's land, labor, and quality of life for generations. (Taken from the Richmond Our Power Coalition's definition of Just Transition.)

Richmond's just transition is not guided by one plan. This library brings together city, community, and regional plans that show how Richmond can move away from fossil fuels while building healthier neighborhoods, good jobs, community ownership, and greater economic justice.

Explore the resources below to see what has already been planned, where different efforts connect, and what still needs support to move forward.

Read more

Richmond residents and environmental justice leaders have worked for decades to protect the community from industrial pollution and build a healthier local economy. Their organizing helped lead to solar job programs, local hiring rules, clean-energy investments, housing protections, reentry services, community-owned development, and stronger public health policies.

Events such as the 2012 Chevron refinery fire and the severe wildfire smoke of 2020 made the need for change even more urgent. In response, residents, community organizations, labor partners, the City, and regional groups created new plans, studies, campaigns, and projects focused on Richmond's future.

This library brings those efforts together. Some resources cover the whole city, while others focus on housing, transportation, health, climate, workforce development, community ownership, or refinery transition.

Not every resource uses the words "just transition." Together, however, they identify many of the projects, policies, funding options, workforce strategies, and decision-making structures Richmond may need. They also name risks that must be avoided, including displacement, pollution, exclusion from decisions, and green development that repeats old inequalities.

Our Future Economy uses these plans alongside community workshops and residents' ideas. This helps show which projects already have both community support and a foundation in public planning — and where more funding, coordination, and accountability are still needed.

How we chose these resources: we included plans, reports, and tools that do at least one of the following: advance environmental, health, worker, racial, or economic justice; identify projects and policies for Richmond's future economy; reflect community or labor participation; or provide research, funding ideas, governance models, and other tools that can help move projects forward.

Richmond Green & Blue New Deal

Commissioned by the City of Richmond · researched and written by Appraccel

Focus

A community-informed plan identifying prioritized projects that can create 1,000 blue-green sector jobs in Richmond as part of a broader just transition strategy. This work comes out of the 2021 Resolution 88-21 to create a Richmond Green-Blue New Deal.

Planning Process

The plan was developed in partnership between the city, outside consultants, and over 40 community partners. A nine-month community engagement process identified community priorities, barriers, and opportunities for Richmond's green-blue economy through surveys, interviews, stakeholder meetings, and workshops. Findings from this process informed the plan's recommendations and implementation materials.

Current & Upcoming

As of June 2025, five priority projects have been identified through city staff surveys, with defined next steps to guide implementation over the next five years. These five projects have been approved by the City Council.

EBMUD Climate Action Plan

East Bay Municipal Utility District (EBMUD)

Focus

A utility-focused climate action plan that evaluates climate risks and identifies mitigation and adaptation strategies to ensure reliable, high-quality water and wastewater services across Alameda and Contra Costa counties. The plan addresses system-wide vulnerabilities, greenhouse gas reduction targets, renewable energy strategies, and long-term infrastructure resilience.

Planning Process

The plan builds on prior EBMUD policies and planning efforts, including the Climate Change Monitoring & Response Plan (2014), Wastewater Climate Change Plan (2019), and Urban Water Management Plan. It incorporates climate projections, system vulnerability assessments, and operational analyses to identify mitigation and adaptation strategies across EBMUD services.

Plan published January 2021.

Current & Upcoming

The plan outlines both completed actions and ongoing initiatives, including targets to eliminate greenhouse gas emissions in the water system by 2030 and reduce emissions in the wastewater system by 2040. Implementation is ongoing through infrastructure investments, renewable energy expansion, and regional partnerships.

Climate Smart Cities Richmond

City of Richmond and The Trust for Public Land · input from a technical advisory team of agencies, city staff, and community organizations · updated in 2025 with Third Plateau and other partners

Focus

A web-based climate planning and implementation tool that helps the City and community partners analyze and prioritize climate projects, drive local investments, and pursue climate funding. The tool combines technical data and community-informed criteria to identify areas most vulnerable to climate impacts and support multi-benefit projects.

Planning Process

Building on the City's policy and program alignment work focused on health and racial equity, from 2016 to 2017 the City of Richmond, Trust for Public Land, and an advisory group built this tool, incorporating local factors like unemployment, vehicle ownership, and public health indicators alongside environmental justice data to understand which communities may be most affected by flooding, sea level rise, and poor air quality.

The planning process also looked at which places are most important to protect during emergencies — hospitals, fire and police services, utilities, schools, recreation centers, and other community facilities — helping shape the tool to guide future climate investments where they're needed most.

Current & Upcoming

City Plan

Richmond Health in All Policies Progress Report 2020

Commissioned by the City of Richmond · prepared by Professor Jason Corburn and researchers at the UC Berkeley Center for Global Healthy Cities

Focus

A citywide progress report tracking how Richmond's Health in All Policies ordinance is advancing health equity across government. The report looks at how city policies, programs, and partnerships affect the social and environmental conditions that shape health, with a focus on reducing structural racism, toxic stressors, and long-standing inequities.

Planning Process

This progress report builds on Richmond's Health in All Policies ordinance, drafted by the Richmond Health Equity Partnership and adopted in 2014, and on a strategy document developed with city departments, community-based organizations, and health and social justice partners. For this 2020 report, the city and UC Berkeley used data and interviews — drawing on the American Community Survey, CalEnviroScreen, California Health Interview Survey, police data, Transparent Richmond, the Richmond Community Survey, and school district data, plus interviews with city staff, community organizations, and residents — to measure progress across the same six Health in All Policies intervention areas, comparing newer data with earlier years and paying particular attention to differences by neighborhood, race and ethnicity, and income.

Timeline

The HiAP ordinance was adopted in April 2014. The report was published in 2020, mainly using data from roughly 2013–2019, with some longer trend comparisons reaching back to the 2000s and an overview of conditions as of February 2020, before COVID-19.

Current & Upcoming

The report outlined next-step recommendations. A 2020 Evaluation from the Health in All Policies Interdepartmental Working Group defined further next steps for Richmond HiAP on the City's open data website, Transparent Richmond, in October 2022 — including strategic intervention areas, key findings from the most recent Progress Report, recommended next steps, and case studies highlighting the need for and importance of Health in All Policies.

Community Plan

North Richmond Quality of Life Plan

Developed by and for North Richmond residents. Led by the North Richmond Resident Leadership Team (RLT), with administrative support from Healthy Richmond and Richmond Neighborhood Housing Services (RNHS)

Focus

A neighborhood-level plan outlining how resident-led advocacy and community priorities can shape equitable development in North Richmond, an unincorporated community in Contra Costa County. The plan addresses affordable housing, economic opportunity, youth and education, and community safety, with an explicit focus on preventing displacement and gentrification and ensuring longtime residents receive the benefits of new development.

Planning Process

The plan began after residents learned that the Housing Authority of Contra Costa County planned to redevelop Las Deltas, a historic affordable housing site in North Richmond. Residents organized to ensure redevelopment reflected community priorities and protected existing residents.

Healthy Richmond began organizing in May 2018, and the ten-member Resident Leadership Team launched in July 2018, conducting 127 relational interviews and eight stakeholder focus groups. In October 2018, more than 80 residents attended a community visioning event at Verde Elementary School, and five public planning sessions from November 2018 to March 2019 refined priorities into the plan. Institutional stakeholders were invited to join a "Leadership Circle" to identify sponsors, implementing partners, and resources.

Timeline

Organizing began May 2018. The Resident Leadership Team launched July 2018, followed by interviews and focus groups over summer 2018. A community visioning event was held October 2018, and public planning sessions ran November 2018–March 2019. The plan was released March 30, 2019. Early action projects were scheduled for summer 2019, with longer-term priorities extending through 2029 and beyond.

Current & Upcoming

The plan identifies early implementation work around housing advocacy, renter outreach, homeownership education, youth and community center planning, traffic safety, community benefits, public art, local history, and gateway signage. Key next steps include advocating for at least 50% affordable units in new development, outreach to renters, youth surveys for a future community center, traffic safety proposals, a resident-led community benefits campaign, and activation of a North Richmond Specific Plan aligned with Envision Contra Costa 2040.
